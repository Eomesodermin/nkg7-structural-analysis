NKG7 Hetero-Complex Screen — Does NKG7 Dock onto a Ca²⁺ Partner?

Project: NKG7 structural analysis (proj_d2fe638c3a6f) · Stage: post-Tier-1 pivot (hetero-complex) · Date: 2026-07-14/15 · Cluster: marvin (Uni Bonn)
Query: NKG7 (UniProt Q16617, 165 aa) · Partners: ATP6AP2 (O75787), ORAI1 (Q96D31), STIM1 (Q13586) · Predictors: Boltz-2, AlphaFold3 3.0.1, Chai-1 0.6.1 (1 A40 GPU each)

Result: no NKG7–partner complex reaches a confident interface — and, decisively, neither does the known ATP6AP2 binder.
Across all three predictors and all three partners, the best cross-chain interface confidence (ipTM) is 0.59 (AlphaFold3 · NKG7–STIM1), well below the ~0.8 confident bar. Critically, the positive control — ATP6AP2, a published NKG7 interactor — also scores weakly (0.10–0.33). Because the method fails to recover a known interaction, the weak ORAI1/STIM1 scores are uninformative negatives (the assay lacks sensitivity for these targets), not evidence that the interactions are absent.

1. Why this screen

Tier 1 showed NKG7 does not form a self-contained ion channel: no confident homo-oligomer encloses a conducting pore (see the Tier-1 report). But NKG7's whole protein family — the CaV/TARP-γ (CACNG) auxiliary subunits and GSG1L — works by docking onto a partner channel and regulating it, not by conducting. And NKG7 loss reduces Ca²⁺ influx in CD8⁺ T cells. So the better-posed question is not "is NKG7 a pore?" but "what does NKG7 bind, and does it thereby modulate Ca²⁺ indirectly?"

We co-folded NKG7 with three candidate partners, each chosen for a specific reason:

  • ATP6AP2 — a known NKG7 binder (v-ATPase accessory subunit; the NKG7→v-ATPase→mTORC1 axis). This is the positive control: the method must recover it for any negative to be interpretable.
  • ORAI1 — the pore-forming subunit of the CRAC channel, the dominant Ca²⁺-influx pathway in T cells. The primary hypothesis for NKG7's Ca²⁺ phenotype.
  • STIM1 — the ER Ca²⁺ sensor that gates ORAI1; tests association with the CRAC activation machinery.

Reading. Only two values clear the 0.5 floor — AF3 · STIM1 (0.59) and Boltz-2 · ORAI1 (0.45, with an overall ipTM of 0.54) — and no partner clears it across more than one predictor. The three predictors do not agree on a preferred partner (Boltz favours ORAI1, AF3 favours STIM1, Chai is flat-low), the signature of no well-defined complex. Most importantly, ATP6AP2 — the known binder — is not recovered confidently by any predictor, which sets the sensitivity floor of this assay: it cannot presently distinguish "no interaction" from "an interaction it can't model." Under that floor, the ORAI1/STIM1 results are inconclusive, not negative.

3. Interface geometry

The predicted interfaces are large and diffuse — 61–75 NKG7 residues in contact for each partner — and NKG7 presents nearly the same surface (N-terminus, TM1, inter-TM loops) to all three partners rather than a partner-specific patch. Both are hallmarks of low-confidence models where the chains settle together without a defined binding mode, consistent with the low ipTM.

One nuance worth recording: in the ATP6AP2 model, NKG7 does contact ATP6AP2's transmembrane helix (res 303–318) and cytoplasmic tail, which is membrane-plausible and consistent with the published TM-mediated interaction. So the positive control docks in a reasonable location but with low confidence — the failure is one of confidence, not gross mislocalisation. That is a softer negative than a wrong-place docking would be, and leaves the door open that a better-resolved method (or the true multi-subunit context) would score it higher.

4. Interpretation & gate

Gate: inconclusive — do not over-read the negatives, and do not escalate blindly. The screen does not support a confident NKG7–ORAI1, NKG7–STIM1, or even NKG7–ATP6AP2 binary complex. But because the positive control failed the confidence bar, the correct conclusion is that pairwise co-folding is under-powered for NKG7's partnerships, not that NKG7 binds nothing. This is itself a useful, honest result: it says the binary-complex prediction route has hit its resolution limit here.

Why binary co-folding may be the wrong tool

  • NKG7 self-associates (western blot + pulldown; corroborated by a reproducible predicted dimer interface). Its physiological partner may be a NKG7 oligomer, not a monomer — a binary 1:1 co-fold cannot capture an avidity-driven or oligomer-presented interface.
  • The real targets are multi-subunit. CRAC is a hexameric ORAI1 assembly gated by STIM1 clusters; the v-ATPase is a large multi-subunit machine. A γ-subunit-like regulator often binds the assembled complex, not an isolated chain.
  • Membrane context is absent. These are soluble co-folds; a lipid bilayer constrains TM–TM docking that the predictors approximate poorly.

5. Recommended next steps

  1. Higher-order co-folds: NKG7 + ORAI1 hexamer, and NKG7 dimer + ORAI1, to test oligomer/avidity-presented interfaces the 1:1 fold misses.
  2. Experimental partner discovery over prediction: given the sensitivity floor, an unbiased co-IP / proximity-labelling (BioID/APEX) mass-spec screen in cytotoxic lymphocytes would be far more decisive than more co-folding — it also directly leverages the pulldown assays already run.
  3. Re-fold any experimental hit from that screen (that is the regime where structure prediction earns its keep — a real hit to model, not a blind pairwise sweep).

6. Methods

NKG7 (chain A) was co-folded with each partner (chain B) using three predictors on marvin, one A40 GPU per predictor, all three complexes bundled per job. Boltz-2: --use_msa_server --recycling_steps 3 --diffusion_samples 5 --no_kernels. Chai-1 0.6.1: --use-msa-server --no-use-esm-embeddings --num-diffn-samples 5 (ESM weights not fetchable on the compute node). AlphaFold3 3.0.1: full MSA data pipeline per complex, apptainer container. Interface confidence = cross-chain ipTM (off-diagonal of the per-chain-pair ipTM matrix) of the best model per predictor. Interfaces computed at a 5 Å heavy-atom cutoff (MDAnalysis). Partner sequences: UniProt canonical full-length.

7. Limitations

  • Binary 1:1 co-folds only; no higher-order assemblies, no membrane, no dynamics.
  • The positive control (ATP6AP2) was not recovered confidently, so the assay's negative predictive value here is low — negatives are inconclusive by construction.
  • Full-length partners include large soluble domains (ATP6AP2 ectodomain, STIM1 cytoplasmic coiled-coil) that add noise to a TM-mediated docking question.
  • All results are computational; partner identification requires experimental validation (co-IP/MS, proximity labelling).

8. Glossary

Hetero-complex
A complex of two different proteins (vs. a homo-oligomer of identical copies).
ipTM (interface pTM)
Predicted TM-score of the inter-chain interface (0–1); a model-confidence proxy for whether two chains are correctly docked. ~0.8 = confident, <0.5 = not credible.
Cross-chain ipTM
The off-diagonal (A↔B) entry of the per-chain-pair ipTM matrix — the confidence specifically of the NKG7–partner interface, isolating it from within-chain confidence.
Positive control
A case with a known answer (here, the published NKG7–ATP6AP2 interaction) used to calibrate whether the method can detect a true signal at all.
CRAC channel / ORAI1 / STIM1
Ca²⁺-release-activated Ca²⁺ channel; ORAI1 is the hexameric pore, STIM1 the ER Ca²⁺ sensor that gates it — the main Ca²⁺-influx route in T cells.
ATP6AP2
(Pro)renin receptor / v-ATPase accessory protein; a documented NKG7 interactor (NKG7→v-ATPase→mTORC1 axis).
CaV/TARP-γ (CACNG)
Auxiliary, non-conducting tetraspan subunits that regulate Ca²⁺ and AMPA channels — NKG7's structural family, all partner-docking regulators.
